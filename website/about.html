About LiftBoi

LiftBoi is what you get when you take a perfectly good open-source gym tracker, pop the hood, and go "yeah, I'm keeping the engine — everything else is getting replaced."

Before a single line of tracking-app code was mine, I already had eleven training programs' worth of methodology sitting in my head: Mentzer's HIT, Israetel and Hoffmann's volume landmarks, Nippard's Powerbuilding and Min-Max, the Dysfunctional Lifter's Handbook — all of it synthesized into one library, autoregulated top sets and RPE drift and weekly volume climbs and everything. What I didn't have was a tracker that could hold all of that without turning into a spreadsheet with delusions of grandeur.

So I went looking for a skeleton, not a start-from-scratch project, and openGym had already solved the boring-but-hard 80%: self-hosted, passkey login, nobody's server but mine, a 1,324-exercise library, a muscle map that actually tells you something instead of just looking impressive. I wasn't about to rebuild any of that for the sake of it. I forked it, kept the organs that worked, and started replacing the rest with my own.

The actual reason I went self-hosted in the first place is embarrassingly simple: subscriptions. Every tracker worth using wanted a monthly fee to unlock features that are, structurally, a JSON array and some arithmetic. I was not going to pay rent on my own workout log. openGym's entire existence is a rebuttal to that business model, which made it the only sane thing to build on.

So that's LiftBoi: the eleven-program library wearing a Solo Leveling coat of violet paint, running on someone else's very good foundation. Everything under the hood that isn't mine is still openGym's, and it's staying AGPL — because that's the license that stops the next person from doing to me what I just did to Duarte, except with a paywall bolted on.

— 5anku

Principles

  • Your data is yours. Local-first, one-file backups, no lock-in. Leaving LiftBoi must always be one export away.
  • No telemetry, ever. If a feature needs analytics to justify itself, it does not ship.
  • Dependency-light. The backend is one Node file with a single dependency; the frontend is React, a router and a store. Small enough to actually audit.
  • Open source that stays open. AGPL-3.0, so every fork and every hosted version owes its code back to everyone — this one included.
